The offside law

A player is offside if, at the moment a teammate plays or touches the ball, they're in an offside position and they get involved in play. Being in an offside position on its own isn't an offence.

Offside position

  • You're in an offside position if any part you can score with (head, body, feet) is nearer the goal line than both the ball and the second-last opponent. The keeper usually counts as the last one.
  • Level is onside. Level with the second-last defender, or level with the ball, is fine.
  • Arms and hands don't count. An arm ahead of the line isn't offside.
  • You can't be offside in your own half.
  • A defender who steps off the pitch still counts as standing on their goal line.

When it becomes an offence

Playing the ballTouching or playing a pass from a teammate.
Blocking the keeperStanding in their line of sight, or challenging them for the ball.
Gaining an advantagePlaying a rebound off the post, the bar or a save.

When it's never offside

  • Straight from a throw-in, a corner or a goal kick.
  • When the ball comes off a defender who deliberately played it. A block, deflection or save doesn't count as deliberate, so offside still applies then.
  • A player who was offside but stayed out of it, not touching the ball or affecting anyone.

It's all judged the moment the ball is played, not when the player receives it.

How Flag Up works

  • Watch the clip at full speed. You get one replay.
  • Call it: Flag up (no goal) or Play on (goal stands).
  • A right call scores 10 points, and each one in a row adds 10 more, up to 50. A wrong call resets your streak.
  • Every 5th case is super hard: faster, and worth double.
  • Daily 10: the same 10 calls for everyone, new at midnight UK time, one go each.
  • Unlimited: calls keep coming and keep getting faster. Three wrong and you're out.
  • Real cases are famous pre-VAR calls. Player positions are approximate.
